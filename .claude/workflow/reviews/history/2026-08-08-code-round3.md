## Attack pass (beyond the plan's list)

- **Cross-seam money/entitlement divergence** — the highest-value attack this round and it landed: finding 1. Whenever a payment settles through anything other than the customer's own returning browser, the plan the money bought is discarded.
- **Falsy-parameter erasure at a service boundary** — finding 2. Both the Python and JS clients drop `""`, so a validated enum becomes an unvalidated default.
- **Stale row beats frozen intent** — finding 3. The intent was made authoritative for pricing but not for applying.
- **Confirm binding** — `_apply` trusts `intent.subscription` for *which* sub but re-checks *whose*; the order id is the intent's frozen handle (`confirm.py:485-491`) and a mismatch opens `RZP_ORDER_MISMATCH`, so a customer cannot aim one subscription's payment at another. Defended.
- **Type confusion / injection / oversize on `target_plan`** — `dict`/`list` refused at the signature boundary; `' OR 1=1 --` and `../../etc/passwd` reach a parameterised docname lookup and are refused; 300×A reflected verbatim (round-2 m12, unchanged). Defended.
- **Double-submit** — the ledger, not the SPA's `busy` ref, is the guard, and it holds under real concurrency (executed).
- **Mandate release on supersede** — an APPLIED intent is now supersedable; `_enqueue_release` returns early on committed money and `release_superseded_object` re-checks under lock. Defended (re-verified live: the fixture's settled signup mandate survived being superseded).
- **Offer/accept resolution divergence** (plan open question 4) — unchanged, out of scope by the plan's own record; now less dangerous than in round 2 because nothing writes the subscription pre-payment.
